# Human review and approval readiness

AI findings are advisory. Review exact revisions and actual supporting passages, not the confidence or polish of a model response.

## Semantic and responsible quality

Check clarity (actors/meaning), completeness (supported needs and open issues), consistency, testability, relevance, transparency, stakeholder coverage, traceability and ethical risk handling. Check inclusion, accessibility, affected people, privacy and fairness only where relevant to supplied context; record missing expertise and do not certify regulatory compliance.

For each substantive commitment, ask: does the cited passage/confirmed decision support the actor, permission, behaviour, quantity, unit, exception and scope? An existing ID is not proof. A citation that contradicts its source remains proposed or is rejected. No numerical threshold is justified merely because a model chose a plausible default.

Criteria must describe observable behaviour and boundary/exception cases as appropriate. Separate business meaning from Mermaid syntax. Check document sections against the selected artifact revisions. Newly generated commitments require support and review, including commitments introduced by summaries or exports.

Record findings with affected references, severity, evidence and owner. Preserve false alarms and human disposition. Review/edit/select/reject proposals explicitly; only selected revisions enter drafts. Do not claim that another AI task or a deterministic validator independently validated business fitness.

## Readiness for human approval

Before recording approval, present:

1. Exact artifact manifest (IDs/revisions) and explicit exclusions.
2. Identified authorised human, scope and actual basis for authority.
3. Current input, criterion, model and document dependencies; no pending impact review.
4. Required human reviews and verified semantic support for substantive commitments.
5. Testable criteria or a justified human exception.
6. No unresolved blocking clarification/finding affecting the selected scope.
7. Explicit owner/rationale/disposition for accepted nonblocking uncertainty.
8. Actual human decision and its time/basis, or a clear statement that approval is pending.

Critical missing facts cannot be disguised as accepted assumptions. Deferred blockers remain blockers. A partial baseline excludes unrelated unresolved work explicitly. Document/process dependencies that are part of the deliverable must be included in the approval manifest. Any externally obtained agreement must identify what was agreed and by whom. Local names/roles are not authenticated electronic signatures.

Recognising a risk or finding as confirmed does not resolve it. Record the actual human disposition when resolving, rejecting, excluding or declaring uncertainty not applicable. Accepted nonblocking uncertainty and excluded artifacts must belong to the approval's scope; their retained records explain the decision without becoming approved artifacts.

## Change and recovery

On a changed source answer or changed trace, identify downstream requirements, criteria, process and document dependencies, including trace-only connections. Review impacts before retaining any unaffected status. Update refs to the correct revisions and reverify their meaning. Preserve baseline history; changed working items require revalidation and appropriately scoped new human approval. Restore creates drafts and does not revive authority or approval.

## Assessment versus approval

The nine research dimensions can guide review. A high rubric total does not grant approval or cancel a critical unsupported commitment. Structural validation establishes only the fields/relationships it actually checks. Semantic correctness, business agreement, processing permission and compliance remain human/context-dependent.
